# The desk

The desk (`src/PlayMap.App`) has three workspaces (PLAY, MAP, SCAN), a show strip, an attention bar, four drawers and
generic parts, so most features add data, not panels. It sends verbs and reads STATE: every button,
right-click entry and key names the catalogue line it sends, the same line the wire, MCP and Companion send. A line whose
catalogue row presses hold, as a Companion key holds it, is held to confirm wherever the desk sends it from a button or a menu — the
desk's own controls, a card's line, a generated entry (FAILOVER, a pattern, an output closed or identified, a surface hidden or
given a source, a blind commit, a re-solve, LIGHT WALL, PAIRING NEW among them): its button sends only when held to the end of its
ring, and in a menu it is pressed twice — the first press arms the entry, which then reads "Press again to send <line>" and sends
nothing; a second press of it within 3 s of the desk's clock sends that line once (a line a push moved in between arms the entry
afresh, naming the new line); another entry, the menu closing or made again, or the 3 s passing lets it go. Three such rows the
desk sends at every press wherever they are, with the air's controls, by the owner's decision: the transport's Stop (`VIDEO STOP`),
the panic banner's Clear (`PANIC CLEAR`) and the master (`MASTER`); PANIC, Blackout, GO, NEXT, PREV, Play and Pause are held nowhere.
A card's `APPROVE`, which a Companion key presses twice, the desk presses once, also by the owner's decision. An inspector's field
and a drag send their line as they change. Menu entries show their words as they are (underscores and all; a menu has no access
keys).

Round 3 makes the desk guided, with every command in its menus; *Where the desk is going*, below, says how.

## Where the desk is going

Round 3 takes the desk there in six packages: desk-ux, desk-gpu, desk-shell, desk-map, canvas and workflows
(`docs/round3/PLAN.md` D11–D16, §3.6, §3.12, §3.17–§3.20). The design is `docs/round3/design/desk-workflows.md`. desk-ux's part is built (the GO guard, blind in grey, the Oops history,
the look and the status shapes, preview age, closing while armed, the quad that stays, map anything: the parts above); the rest is to come.

What proves desk-ux's part today is headless, on both CI jobs: `GoGuardTests` (the border for the node's MinGo, a flash at most every
500 ms, the red border under the pointer), `BlindLookTests`, `StatusRuleTests` (every light's shape and word, in templates and on the
canvases; each light 3:1 against every theme's panels), `PreviewAgeTests`, `CloseArmedTests`, `MapAnythingTests` and the `Quad*Tests`
with `PinNudgeTests`. **L19 is not run** (no lab: the owner's answer 5): the formative dark-room session with three operators
(`docs/round3/lab/L19.md`) waits for a lab, and with it whether operators in a dark room read the guard, the shapes and the stale badge,
reach PANIC under pressure, and find their place and a faulted output after a desk restart.

- **Guides beside the tools.** Simple opens on a workflows home, a card per guide: two projectors blended or stacked on a flat
  screen; a film mapped onto a wall so it reads undistorted from the audience, blendable to more projectors; an object mapped with
  one or two projectors; First light; the facade. A guide docks its step rail beside the live view and shows only the fields each
  step uses, each step with Do it, By hand, Ask and Explain. The engine computes the steps (`GUIDE`), so Patterns, Companion and an
  assistant read the same ones. Pro shows every pane, the advanced panes and the console (`SimpleProTests`).
- **Every command in the menus.** A menu bar (File, Edit, View, Map, Output, Show, Guides, Window, Help) holds every row the desk may
  send and every desk action. Right-click menus are generated from the catalogue, with Copy line and Ask about this…, and the Ctrl+K
  palette searches the same entries. A free value is typed in the entry's own field, so no line needs typing (`MenuCoverageTests`).
  A console (Ctrl+\`) types any line through the desk's gate and refuses a line that carries a secret.
- **Panes.** The outliner, the inspector, the bin, the cards, the drawers, the assistant, the console and the journal dock, float
  inside the desk's window (never onto an output) and collapse to a rail that keeps the title and the worst light. A layout is kept
  for each workspace and each guide. The show strip, the attention bar, PANIC, Blackout and the mode frame stay fixed. The advanced
  blocks above (effects, modulation and tempo, bindings and learn, timecode, LED, optimise, live inputs and sends, align, the facade,
  sync) each get an area pane in Pro, and the effects chain and modulation and bindings get editors of their own.
- **Colour.** Status, identity (which projector, output or client, with its number badge) and workflow stage each keep colours of
  their own, and none is the only cue.
- **Safety and previews.** Built (desk-ux, the parts above): the GO guard's border and single flash, blind in grey with BLIND on
  every pane that edits the draft, the Oops panel (`UNDO LIST`, `UNDO TO`), the dark room and daylight themes with a desk
  brightness, every light's shape and word, and each preview's age. To come (desk-gpu, §3.12): where the desk shares the engine's
  adapter, a preview is a shared texture with no CPU copy; everywhere else the ring stays.
- **The quad and every handle.** Built (desk-ux): the quad that stays as a draft per projector, its corners dragged, typed and
  nudged, a pin typed and nudged, and First light reopened from the view's right-click and the help overlay (MAP's quad tool,
  above). To come (desk-map, §3.18): every other handle by one pixel, with the engine rows an encoder or an assistant uses.
- **What each screen shows.** Every view of an output names what it shows now, from STATE `outputs[].showing`.
- **One canvas.** One pattern, media or generator spans several outputs as one canvas (`CANVAS SPAN`): side by side, stacked, or
  both.
- **By hand, and with the assistant.** Every mapping step can be done by hand. An automatic result is a layer the hand's edits sit
  on, or a value a re-run stages rather than overwrites, so a re-run never overwrites a hand edit unasked. The assistant's pane
  holds the conversation and each proposed line with Show me, Approve and Reject; its lines pass the same gate as a person's.

## The spine other desk packages build on

`DeskModule` (slots, banners, shortcuts, menu extras, badges, inspector sections, drawer tabs, the gate, an Esc rung),
composed by one partial hook per package (`MainViewModel.Spine|Show|Content|Map.cs`), each push taken in two passes (every module
takes it, the gate's facts move, then every module re-reads its actions, so none asks the gate with the push before); an action
raises only what moved; views registered with `ModuleViews`;
`StateSnapshot.Root` and `Section<T>` (read once per snapshot); `IMenuTarget` on every row and inspector; `DeskSession.Gate`
and `DeskSession.Verbs`; `StatusRow`, `HoldButton`, `ScoreRing`, `DeskDrop`; the param templates in
`Theme/ParamTemplates.axaml` with a socket slot; `Theme/Desk1|2|3.axaml`. `DeskVerbRuleTests` walks every reachable action
with every fixture loaded and fails on a line the catalogue does not parse, a line with a secret, or a toolbar not in its
menu.

## The top bar

The bar is one row when everything fits and the window is at least 1366 px; otherwise two rows (identity, workspaces and
drawers; then transport, timecode, tempo and the show cluster). With the full show cluster one row wants about 1966 px,
so a 1920 px window shows two rows. Below 1280 px the transport drops its position column and the drawer pills are icons;
below 1200 px Simple|Pro leaves the bar (Ctrl+Shift+P still switches). No child ends beyond the window at 1100, 1280,
1366, 1600 or 1920 px; the show cluster never shrinks. The mode frame takes no room, so arming changes nothing in the bar.
