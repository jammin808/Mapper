# Roadmap

*A prioritised roadmap for PlayMap. Effort and impact are **estimates**. Evidence for each item is in docs/COMPETITIVE.md, docs/UX.md and docs/XR.md. Time targets live in one place: docs/UX.md §5.0.*

## 0. Where we are, and the rule that governs everything

**Merged and tested:**
- The ten modules — assistant, calibration, network, mapping maths, media, scanning, projector control, render, vision and the desk.
- The engine host: `PlayMap engine [headless]` composes the real media, render, network and scan backends; the scan runner, projector control and the assistant run inside it. The desk starts and watches this machine's engine, or attaches to a node on the network over the line wire; its demo engine stays for offline use.
- The round-2 work, built on one spine (the model, the catalogue's rows, the engine's hooks, topics and STATE keys): show safety — Show Mode, locks, panic, the master, the GO fence and preflight, with blind edit (one draft per node, diffed in field words, landed as one undo step at a show boundary), sound on the air and fades and dips on a GO by hand (docs/SHOW.md); the sync group — the leader clock slewed, stepped and held, anchors and the group's air carried to followers with each member's receipt, the group's frame lock, vblank reports and the leader's board with its verdict, and `SHOW ARM|DISARM ALL` and `PREFLIGHT ALL` across the group (docs/SYNC.md); outputs — the presenter seam, vblank truth and the frame lock, the Direct3D 11 flip-model path on Windows as an explicit, experimental choice (`PRESENT PATH DXGI`; AUTO is OpenGL) with its fuse, and per-output latency held to the slowest display with the node's air latency (docs/OUTPUT.md); the HAP family on the GPU, written straight into the render context's upload arena, and the decode-path ladder — every decoder saying the path its frames come by, VAAPI frames imported by dmabuf and D3D11VA on the renderer's own device under its one lock where the renderer can take them, else copy-back or software with the words why (docs/DECODE.md); OSC, MIDI, Art-Net and sACN input with bindings (docs/CONTROL.md) and timecode chase (docs/TIMECODE.md); the node's side of Companion (docs/COMPANION.md); ISF effect chains (docs/EFFECTS.md); audio analysis, the beat clock and the modulation bus (docs/AUDIO.md); content — the GPU particle field, raymarched fractals, reactive scenes, stings and looks (docs/CONTENT.md); the wall — pixel quads, the edit overlay with each cursor's latency measured to the frame that draws it, the first map witnessed by the render host, the projector's view through its output's lens, the score, the calibration ledger, and re-solving only the projector that moved, from its pins or from a calibrated camera's look at its dots, its pins and pixel quads re-seated and an automatic blend solved again (docs/CALIBRATION.md); camera auto-align of flat screens, with each output's white and level matched, one output rescanned onto the canvas the others show, a verify pass and a score on every run, and a guard that names a projector whose own geometry or edge blend fights the measured correction (docs/ALIGN.md); fleet health and the board, and a hot backup that mirrors its primary's show, files, clock, transports and air to the frame, takes the air over by epoch with a press (`FAILOVER`), the old primary fencing itself, hands the show back (`FAILBACK CONFIRM`) and lets a group's follower take the lead (`FLEET PROMOTE`) (docs/FLEET.md); LED pixel mapping (docs/LED.md); video in and out over OMT, NDI and Spout (Windows) — live inputs received once, with tally, backups and a loss policy, and sends packed on the GPU that follow the air (docs/VIDEO.md); optimise on import — HAP versions of a clip made by the show's rules with PlayMap's own GPU encoder in a worker process, kept beside the source (docs/OPTIMISE.md); facades — a site frame that keeps millimetres in a survey's coordinate system, survey import of OBJ, PLY, glTF, LAS, E57 and XYZ off the control lane (decimated or thinned, one undo step), total-station control points that check the model and give pins their points, long-throw solves with lens and position priors that lock the zoom from the lens when the pins cannot carry it (only a lock the lens put there asked again), sites named by their EPSG system with geodesy from the published series, projector stacks aligned to their master's picture from one camera place and colour-matched through the ledger, blended as one projector, the rest of the building dimmed to a stack's remaining light when a member fails, the light plan and the facade guide (docs/FACADE.md, docs/MAPPING.md); and scans from many places — views added `INTO` a scan from a capture camera, a phone or a folder a tethered camera or a person shoots into, every view of every projector solved together (holding the projectors that did not move, dropping a view that disagrees, carried by a move of the site frame), a depth map per projector, one stage mesh and regions joined across projectors, the scan's views and solve kept on disk and read back after a restart, and its measured regions suggested as surfaces that only a person's `APPROVE` makes (docs/SCAN-MULTIVIEW.md, docs/PHONESCAN.md, docs/wire/multiscan.md).

That work spans items from Rounds 1–4 and the mid term; a **Built** note on an item below says what main has of it. Camera auto-align and the content package have no item of their own.

**In flight:** the direct-sampling proof for D3D11VA surfaces and the lab runs of the Direct3D 11 path, Spout and hardware decode on real GPUs; the Companion module; the desk's controls for the new verbs (the show strip, panic, locks, generated right-click entries, cards for every package's STATE). Until the desk's controls land, the new verbs run over the wire, HTTP, MCP and bindings.

**CI:** every push builds the whole solution with the analyzers on and runs every test project on Linux (Mesa llvmpipe, FFmpeg 9.0, no libva and no system OpenAL, as on a bare machine); on Windows it runs the suites that need no GPU or FFmpeg and the Windows-only render and media code on WARP (the DXGI presenter, Spout's round trip, the D3D11VA device), and publishes the portable exe.

**Not yet run on real hardware:** the tests run on Mesa llvmpipe under Xvfb, on the scan, align and stage simulators and on a scripted frame-lock board; OMT runs for real over loopback, and NDI against a fake runtime the tests compile; the NVAPI tier, WASAPI, WinMM, the DXGI flip path, Spout and D3D11VA are compiled on Linux and run by the Windows CI job, which has no GPU (WARP); VAAPI decode compiles here (no VA driver) and its dmabuf import half runs on Mesa; a sync group's nodes run in one process on loopback, on a manual clock with synthetic vblanks. A real projector, a real camera scan (from one place or many, through a capture camera, a phone or a tethered camera's folder), a camera re-solve and a colour match through a real camera, a Panasonic's answers to the double-correction guard's reads, a sync board, HAP R and HAP HDR files from other encoders, real LED nodes, a real NDI runtime, scanner E57 and LAS files and a total station's export, a projector solved at 100 m, a DXGI start, its frame statistics and independent flip on NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs, D3D11VA and VAAPI decode imported on real GPUs (and the device lock under a decoder's load), Spout with Resolume and TouchDesigner, per-output latency on displays of different lag, and a second machine (the video checklist in docs/VIDEO.md among them) are lab checks still to do.

**The rule:** **Round 0** on real hardware is the gate. Every lens agreed: it is the cheapest way to learn which design assumptions are wrong.

## 1. How we chose the order

1. **First light, then smoothness, then interop, then show-floor discipline.** Nothing else matters if the wall stutters.
2. **Serve 3D mapping artists, corporate AV and installs first.** Serve VJs early through interop so PlayMap can be the mapper behind Resolume or TouchDesigner; build the instrument layer mid-term.
3. **Every feature ships as verbs:** a verb with typed parameters, a STATE key, a model field and a `DeskAction`, enforced in CI.
4. **Measure before we claim.** Publish scan time, RMS, sync offset, layer counts and latency per release. Until then, every "better than" is a hypothesis.

Decisions where the lenses disagreed are in docs/COMPETITIVE.md §8. In short: D3D11 before any pro release, not before first light; no timeline engine; `PICK`/`HIGHLIGHT`/`CURSOR` in Round 1 and the phone in Round 3; risk gating depends on the caller; Spout and OMT always available, NDI as a plugin; 10-bit in Round 2, HDR signalling mid-term; macOS desk mid-term, engine long-term.

## 5. Non-technical track: sustainability and adoption

These are the biggest adoption risks and no code fixes them. Rental houses and integrators buy support, training and confidence, not just software.

| Item | Why | When |
|---|---|---|
| Second maintainer and a documented release process | A single-maintainer project has a bus factor of one; MapMap shows what unmaintained looks like | Before the first pro release |
| Documentation site with workflows, the hardware list and the network guide | Bring-your-own-hardware needs a buyer's guide | Round 4 |
| Support offer (paid SLA option), training and a certification path | What rental houses and integrators actually buy | After Round 4 |
| Funding model | Pays for the lab, signing certificates and support | Decide before Round 4 |
| Freedom-to-operate review of calibration patents (Christie, Scalable, VIOSO/domeprojection and others) and model licences | "Detect and propose" is a product choice, not a legal conclusion | Before a commercial release |
| Community: forum, example projects, public benchmark (M32) | Trust comes from reproducible numbers | Ongoing |

## 6. Skip or watch

- AV2, VVC, LCEVC, ProRes RAW and 4D splats: no hardware, or a camera/OTT concern (docs/COMPETITIVE.md §9).
- Dante AV video natively: ingest through capture cards instead.
- A full timeline engine: replaced by TC-locked playlists and per-item keyframes.
- Per-frame neural compensation on show nodes: lab-grade and single-projector ([CGF survey](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cgf.70288)).
- Automatic content-based correction without human approval. Christie holds patents in this area, including drift correction from reduced pattern sets ([US20200092524](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20200092524)). We detect and propose, and a human approves; whether that is clear of any patent needs the review in §5.

## 7. Summary by group

Main has R0.1, R1.1, R1.2, R1.3, R2.3, R3.1, R3.2, R3.3, R3.5 and R3.7 in code (each but what its row names), and R2.1, R2.6, R2.10, R3.14, R4.1 (a manual takeover), R4.2 and M3 in part, and M23 in part (the site frame, survey import of PLY, OBJ, glTF, LAS and E57 with decimation and thinning, control points, long-throw priors, geodesy, stacks, the light plan and the facade guide; not LAZ, the night profile, photometry or glass masks); what they lack is proof on real hardware.

**Differentiators where PlayMap can lead:** commodity-camera calibration with an explainable score (R1.4, R4.8); the edit overlay drawn on the wall (R1.5); pick-and-highlight verbs (R1.12); click-to-surface (R1.7, M30); optimise to HAP R/HDR (R2.4); measured sync and latency (R2.7, R1.11); laser and phone pointing (R3.10, R3.9); importers and designer templates (R3.12, R3.13); live-mirror redundancy for deterministic content (R4.1); rework-free recalibration (R1.2, R4.7); drift watch (M2); LED auto-locate (M5); geometry-aware instruments (M8); staged AI on a live show (R3.5 + assistant); camera glasses and headsets (M29, M10). Main has R1.4, R1.5 and R4.8 in large part, R1.2's rework-free re-solve of a moved projector from a camera (its pixel quads re-seated; not R4.7's retargeting to a changed mesh), R2.4 in part (HAP and HAP Q, not HAP R or HDR), R2.7 in part (the vblank board, not the camera's certificate), R1.11 in part (each output's input lag held to the slowest display, not the camera's measure), R4.1 for a manual takeover, and camera auto-align of flat screens, with its white match, beside them.

**Moonshots:** L1–L3. Glasses as the way to run a mapped show, and a show that keeps itself aligned.

## 8. Measures of done, per release

Each is measured against the canonical targets in docs/UX.md §5.0 and published:
- Time to first light; time to map a box with a tested webcam.
- RMS per solve on the lab objects; the public benchmark (M32) once it exists.
- Two-node sync offset p50/p99.
- Layer counts per GPU and codec.
- 72-hour soak missed vblanks.
- Failover: frame identity and end-to-end switch time.
- Bump-to-realigned time.
- Laser, phone and pinch-to-outline latency p50/p95.

Published numbers count as features. They are what a show programmer asks for first.
